Sunnyside Environmental School

Service learning, environmental education and place-based learning are the cornerstones of Sunnyside Environmental School’s curriculum. They are woven into every student’s week, from the school garden to the State Capitol. Our program deeply integrates service learning with storyline methodology, field study and outdoor learning. The excellence of our teaching staff and learning environment are essential to SES’s success in building belonging, engagement, and impact. Since its founding as the Environmental Middle School in 1994, Sunnyside has spent 32 years fostering deeply rooted connections between students and the places they live.

Since 1994

Lifetime impact of the program

For more than three decades, Sunnyside students have carried what they learn into the places where decisions get made.

  • Shaping local and state policy

    Students have testified before Portland City Council in support of the city’s ban on new fossil fuel infrastructure, before the Oregon Fish and Wildlife Commission on endangered status for orcas, and to state lawmakers on the Elliott State Forest and HB 4134. A second-grade campaign, “Loam not Foam,” pushed to replace polystyrene lunch trays and was part of the movement toward Oregon’s school polystyrene ban.

  • Taking their case to state leaders

    An 8th-grade cohort worked with local scientists and advocates to produce a video on the proposed Jordan Cove LNG terminal and delivered it to Governor Kate Brown, the State Treasurer and senators. Students also attended Our Children’s Trust hearings in Eugene and Portland in support of youth climate plaintiffs.

  • Teaching beyond our walls

    Middle schoolers wrote a curriculum on local wildlife and brought it to dozens of PPS elementary schools. Sunnyside’s 2013 Energy Teach-In, which brought experts from across the region to help students weigh the world’s energy choices, drew coverage from the Portland Tribune and the Zinn Education Project.

  • Alumni who keep leading

    Graduates include author, activist and scientist Brook Thompson, and recent alumni interned with Environment Oregon and the Hawaii Wildlife Fund in summer 2026.

How it works

Place-based learning and service are woven into all aspects of Sunnyside’s curriculum.

Themed years

Each student completes three of four standards-aligned themed years in mixed-grade (6–8) classrooms.

  • Ocean
  • River
  • Forest
  • Mountain

  • An integrated K–8 community

    Weekly buddy time with first, second and third graders; rotating campus stewardship duties; a weekly 60-minute garden class; and service projects across the city and region.

  • A deep partner network

    54 community organizations host our weekly field studies and service projects.
